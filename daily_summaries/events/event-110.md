# NASA Experimental Mars Helicopter Blade Designs Target Longer Flights

NASA researchers are developing experimental rotorcraft blade designs for future Mars helicopters and are using high-fidelity computer simulations and wind tunnel tests to target longer, higher-range flights for next-generation Mars aerial vehicles.

## Industry implication

If the experimental blade designs and test program succeed, they could enable Mars rotorcraft to fly higher and farther and carry larger science payloads, improving aerial science capabilities on future Mars missions; this could influence design choices for future planetary rotorcraft and related suppliers in the medium term.

## Key facts

- NASA researchers are developing rotorcraft blade designs targeted at longer flights on Mars.
- Engineers are using high-fidelity computer simulations as part of the work.
- Wind tunnel tests are being used to evaluate the blade designs.
- The goal is helicopters that can fly higher and farther while conducting science on Mars, for next-generation Mars flyers.

## Risks

- Results observed in simulations and wind tunnel tests on Earth may not fully translate to performance in the Martian atmosphere.
- Technical challenges remain scaling blade designs for sustained, higher-altitude or longer-duration flight in Mars’s low-density atmosphere.
- Development timelines and transition from experimental designs to flight-qualified hardware are uncertain.
