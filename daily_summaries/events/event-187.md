# NASA to Cover 35th SpaceX Resupply Mission Space Station Departure

NASA announced coverage of the planned departure (undocking) of a SpaceX Dragon spacecraft — the 35th Commercial Resupply Services mission — from the International Space Station no earlier than Wednesday, Nov. 18, with undocking scheduled for 11:05 a.m. EST from the Harmony module’s space-facing port, pending weather, for return to Earth with scientific samples and hardware.

## Industry implication

If completed as scheduled, the event represents the continued operational execution of SpaceX's ISS resupply responsibilities and the routine return of research payloads to NASA and international partners; schedule or recovery issues (e.g., weather) could delay sample return and affect downstream research timelines.

## Key facts

- NASA will cover the departure of a SpaceX Dragon spacecraft (the 35th resupply mission).
- The Dragon is scheduled to depart the International Space Station no earlier than Wednesday, Nov. 18, 2026.
- Undocking is planned for 11:05 a.m. EST from the Harmony module’s space-facing port.
- The spacecraft will return to Earth carrying scientific research samples and hardware.
- The undocking and return are subject to weather conditions.

## Risks

- Weather conditions could delay undocking or return, as noted in the announcement.
- Summary is a pre-event schedule; actual timing and successful return are not confirmed in the source.
