# Amazon files application for direct-to-device satellite constellation

Amazon filed plans with the U.S. Federal Communications Commission for a constellation of more than 5,100 satellites to deliver direct-to-device (D2D) services using Globalstar spectrum.

## Industry implication

If approved and implemented, the proposal would materially expand D2D capacity and competition in satellite communications, could increase the commercial value or regulatory scrutiny of Globalstar's spectrum, and would have long-term implications for operators and device manufacturers in the D2D market. Regulatory approval, spectrum coordination and multi-year deployment make outcomes uncertain.

## Key facts

- Amazon filed plans with the Federal Communications Commission (per the article summary).
- The proposed constellation would include more than 5,100 satellites (per the article summary).
- The constellation would provide direct-to-device (D2D) services (per the article summary).
- The proposal would use Globalstar spectrum (per the article summary).

## Risks

- Regulatory approval from the FCC and any required spectrum authorizations are uncertain.
- Use of Globalstar spectrum may require agreements, coordination, or create disputes that could delay or alter the proposal.
- Deployment of a >5,100-satellite constellation is a multi-year effort subject to technical, supply-chain, and financing risks.
- Increased competition in D2D could pressure existing operators and device ecosystem participants.
