# Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope Launches Aboard SpaceX Falcon Heavy

NASA’s Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope launched successfully aboard a SpaceX Falcon Heavy rocket from Launch Complex 39A at Kennedy Space Center at 7:26 a.m. EDT on Aug. 30, 2026. In the days before liftoff, the telescope was encapsulated in its payload fairing and moved on Aug. 25, 2026, from the Payload Hazardous Servicing Facility to SpaceX’s hangar at Launch Complex 39A for prelaunch processing and mating to the Falcon Heavy. Following launch, the observatory began a roughly three-month, million-mile transfer to its final orbit. NASA had set media coverage plans for prelaunch and launch activities. The Roman telescope is described as a next-generation observatory to explore questions including dark energy and will survey billions of stars and galaxies with a field of view far larger than Hubble’s.

## Industry implication

A successful launch of a flagship NASA observatory on a Falcon Heavy provides near-term business and revenue for SpaceX's heavy-lift capability, reinforces the role of commercial providers for major government science missions, and may strengthen SpaceX’s competitive position in the heavy‑lift market for similar future payloads. The launch also initiates a multi-month transfer and commissioning phase for the Roman observatory, shifting focus to mission operations.

## Key facts

- NASA set media coverage plans for the Roman Space Telescope prelaunch and launch activities.
- NASA and SpaceX targeted Roman's liftoff no earlier than 7:26 a.m. EDT on Sunday, Aug. 30, 2026.
- The Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope launched aboard a SpaceX Falcon Heavy rocket.
- Launch occurred from Launch Complex 39A at Kennedy Space Center, Florida, at 7:26 a.m. EDT on Aug. 30, 2026.
- On Aug. 25, 2026, the telescope was encapsulated in its payload fairing and moved from the Payload Hazardous Servicing Facility to SpaceX’s hangar at Launch Complex 39A as part of prelaunch processing ahead of mating to the Falcon Heavy.
- Following launch, the telescope began a roughly three-month, million-mile transfer to its final orbit.
- NASA described the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope as a next‑generation observatory designed to explore major questions including dark energy.
- The Roman telescope will survey billions of stars and galaxies and has a field of view far larger than Hubble’s.
- The spacecraft is named for Nancy Grace Roman, NASA’s first chief astronomer.

## Risks

- Launch dates stated as targets ('no earlier than') can slip due to technical, range, or weather constraints.
- Prelaunch mating and integration activities at the hangar could reveal issues requiring remediation or additional testing that would have delayed launch.
- Mating and subsequent integration activities could reveal technical or schedule issues that delay the planned launch or require additional testing.
- The mission enters an extended (roughly three-month, million-mile) transfer and commissioning phase during which deployment or system anomalies could delay the start of science operations or degrade performance.
- Any future in-flight or on-orbit anomalies during cruise, deployment, or commissioning could affect mission schedule or performance despite a successful launch.
- On-orbit commissioning or instrument anomalies could affect the mission's scientific return despite a successful launch, and any later identified post-launch problems could create reputational risk for the launch provider.
