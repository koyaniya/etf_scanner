# NASA Sets Coverage for Roman Space Telescope Launch from Florida

NASA and SpaceX are targeting the launch of the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope aboard a SpaceX Falcon Heavy rocket no earlier than 7:26 a.m. EDT on Sunday, Aug. 30, 2026; NASA has set media coverage plans for prelaunch and launch activities. As part of prelaunch processing, the telescope was encapsulated in its payload fairing and moved from the Payload Hazardous Servicing Facility to SpaceX’s hangar at Launch Complex 39A on Aug. 25, 2026, ahead of mating to the Falcon Heavy.

## Industry implication

The scheduled launch of a flagship NASA observatory on a Falcon Heavy represents near-term business for SpaceX's heavy‑lift capability and maintains Falcon Heavy's relevance for major government science missions. The recent encapsulation and transfer to the SpaceX hangar are concrete prelaunch milestones that advance the mission toward integration and launch, implying near-term operational activity and revenue opportunities for SpaceX while signaling continued reliance on commercial launch providers for high‑profile NASA science payloads.

## Key facts

- NASA has set coverage plans for the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope prelaunch and launch activities.
- NASA and SpaceX are targeting the telescope's liftoff no earlier than 7:26 a.m. EDT on Sunday, Aug. 30, 2026.
- The mission is scheduled to launch aboard a SpaceX Falcon Heavy rocket.
- The Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope is described as NASA's next-generation observatory designed to explore major questions including dark energy.
- On Aug. 25, 2026, the Roman Space Telescope, encapsulated in its payload fairing, was moved from the Payload Hazardous Servicing Facility to SpaceX’s hangar at Launch Complex 39A, Kennedy Space Center, as part of prelaunch processing and ahead of mating to the Falcon Heavy.

## Risks

- Launch date is a target ('no earlier than') and could slip due to technical, range, or weather constraints.
- Prelaunch issues with the vehicle, payload, or during mating/integration could delay or scrub the launch.
- Problems discovered during processing at the SpaceX hangar could require additional testing or remediation, affecting launch timing and costs.
- Coverage plans do not guarantee launch execution; schedule changes are common for complex missions.
