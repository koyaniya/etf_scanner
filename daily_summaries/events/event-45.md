# NASA’s Pandora Mission Begins Study of Exoplanets, Host Stars

NASA's Pandora mission has begun making observations of exoplanets and their host stars, marking the start of its science operations to characterize the atmospheres of at least 20 exoplanets.

## Industry implication

The start of Pandora's observations advances exoplanet characterization capabilities and will produce atmospheric datasets (hazes, clouds, water) that can inform future instrument design, science priorities, and comparative studies by both government and commercial astronomy programs. The near-term commercial impact on tracked space companies is limited, but the mission could influence demand for follow-on instruments or data-analysis services in the medium term.

## Key facts

- Pandora is described as NASA’s newest exoplanet mission.
- Pandora is the first satellite to launch through NASA’s Astrophysics Pioneers program.
- The mission is now making observations of worlds beyond our solar system and the stars they orbit.
- Pandora will determine the atmospheric make-up of at least 20 exoplanets, including the presence of hazes, clouds, and water.
