# NASA’s Moon Orbiter Spots New, ‘Once-in-Century’ Moon Crater

NASA’s Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter (LRO) detected a newly formed, unusually large lunar crater — described as a 'once-in-century' event — identified by LRO scientist Robert Wagner from a bright spot surrounded by a dark halo in LRO imagery, indicating recent surface disturbance.

## Industry implication

The detection highlights the ongoing value of orbital lunar imaging for monitoring recent surface change and impact hazards. This can inform hazard assessments and site selection for upcoming lunar missions and demonstrates continued scientific utility of LRO data for lunar exploration planning and space-data analytics.

## Key facts

- NASA’s Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter (LRO) imagery revealed an unusually large bright spot circled by a dark halo on the Moon.
- LRO scientist Robert Wagner noticed the feature while scanning a large lunar map, and the halo implies surface material was recently disturbed.
- The discovery was described in the source as a new crater and characterized as a 'once-in-century' event.

## Risks

- The summary does not provide a date or independent confirmation of the crater’s formation; follow-up analysis could revise the interpretation.
- The discovery is primarily scientific; it has limited immediate commercial or operational impact for space-sector companies.
