# NASA Opens Media Accreditation for SpaceX CRS-35; NASA Also Awards Additional Launch Services and Crew Flights to SpaceX

NASA opened media accreditation for SpaceX’s 35th Commercial Resupply Services mission (CRS-35) to the International Space Station. The cargo mission is scheduled to launch on a Falcon 9, with NASA and SpaceX targeting a launch no earlier than October (per the NASA news release published 2026-09-17). NASA has also published related NASA actions during the same period awarding SpaceX launch services for the StarBurst smallsat (Bandwagon rideshare) and a contract modification adding three crewed flights, indicating multiple recent procurements and mission announcements involving SpaceX.

## Industry implication

The CRS-35 media invitation and mission details reaffirm ongoing execution of NASA’s commercial resupply program with SpaceX and near-term demand for Falcon 9 cargo flights to the ISS. Related NASA announcements awarding a Bandwagon rideshare for the StarBurst smallsat and a contract modification for three additional crewed flights further reinforce continued NASA procurement of SpaceX launch capacity across cargo, smallsat rideshares, and crew transport in the near‑to‑medium term, supporting SpaceX launch cadence and utilization.

## Key facts

- NASA opened media accreditation for the next cargo launch to the International Space Station (CRS-35) — NASA news release published 2026-09-17 titled "NASA Invites Media to SpaceX’s 35th Resupply Launch to Space Station."
- The mission is described as the 35th SpaceX commercial resupply services mission to the International Space Station (CRS-35).
- The CRS-35 mission will lift off on a Falcon 9 rocket.
- NASA and SpaceX are targeting a launch no earlier than October (statement in the 2026-09-17 NASA news release).
- NASA’s science site published an item (2026-10-12) that CRS-35 will carry experiments focused on human and plant health and drug-resistant organisms to the ISS.
- The CRS-35 mission will launch from Cape Canaveral Space Force Station in Florida (per the science.nasa.gov article).
- Separately, NASA selected SpaceX to provide launch services for the StarBurst smallsat, which will fly as a Bandwagon rideshare on a Falcon 9 from Space Launch Complex 40 at Cape Canaveral (NASA news release published 2026-09-17).
- NASA awarded SpaceX a contract modification for three additional crewed missions to continue crew transportation to the International Space Station (NASA release published 2026-09-18).

## Risks

- The launch is currently a target of "no earlier than October", so the schedule may shift or be delayed.
- Routine launch risks (technical issues, weather) could postpone the mission.
- On-orbit or experiment-level failures could limit scientific return from CRS-35 payloads.
- For the StarBurst rideshare and additional crewed flights, schedule slips, manifest changes, regulatory or technical issues, or NASA programmatic/budget changes could alter timing or reduce execution of the awarded services.
