# NASA’s SpaceX Crew-13 Launches

On Oct. 1, 2026, a SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket launched a Crew Dragon (Crew-13) from Space Launch Complex 40 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station. The launch, reported at 11:10 a.m. EDT in the NASA news release, carried four crew members — NASA astronauts Jessica Watkins and Luke Delaney, Canadian Space Agency astronaut Joshua Kutryk, and Roscosmos cosmonaut Sergey Teteryatnikov — to the International Space Station for a science expedition.

## Industry implication

A successful Crew-13 crewed launch reinforces SpaceX's role in human spaceflight and supports continued commercial–government partnerships for crew transport to the ISS, maintaining near-term confidence in SpaceX’s crewed launch reliability and operational cadence.

## Key facts

- Launch vehicle: SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket with a Crew Dragon spacecraft (Crew-13).
- Launch site: Space Launch Complex 40, Cape Canaveral Space Force Station, Florida.
- Event date: Oct. 1, 2026.
- Launch time: 11:10 a.m. EDT (per NASA news release / article summary).
- Mission: Crew-13, crewed mission to the International Space Station for a science expedition.
- Crew members named in sources: NASA astronauts Jessica Watkins and Luke Delaney; Canadian Space Agency astronaut Joshua Kutryk; Roscosmos cosmonaut Sergey Teteryatnikov.
- Primary sources: NASA image/article 'NASA’s SpaceX Crew-13 Launches' and NASA news release 'NASA’s SpaceX Crew-13 Launches to International Space Station.'

## Risks

- Crewed launches carry inherent safety and operational risks that could affect reputation if anomalies occur on future missions.
- Ongoing reliance on commercial providers for crew rotation ties NASA ISS operations to the performance and schedule of private launch providers.
- Geopolitical or international partner issues (e.g., involving Roscosmos) could influence mission operations or public perception (not stated as an issue in the provided sources).
