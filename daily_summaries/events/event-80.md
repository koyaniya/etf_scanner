# NASA’s SpaceX Crew-13 Launches

SpaceX launched the Crew-13 mission — a Falcon 9 rocket with a Dragon spacecraft — from Space Launch Complex 40 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station on Oct. 1, 2026, carrying four crew members to the International Space Station for a science expedition. The launch occurred at 11:10 a.m. EDT, according to the NASA news release.

## Industry implication

A successful crewed launch reinforces SpaceX's role in human spaceflight and supports continued commercial–government partnerships for crew transport to the ISS, maintaining near-term confidence in commercial crew launch reliability and operational cadence.

## Key facts

- Launch vehicle: SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket with Dragon spacecraft (Crew-13).
- Launch site: Space Launch Complex 40, Cape Canaveral Space Force Station, Florida.
- Event date: Oct. 1, 2026.
- Launch time: 11:10 a.m. EDT (per NASA news release).
- Mission: Crew-13, four crew members to the International Space Station for a science expedition.
- Crew members named in the sources: NASA astronauts Jessica Watkins and Luke Delaney; Canadian Space Agency (CSA) astronaut Joshua Kutryk; Roscosmos cosmonaut Sergey Teteryatnikov.
- Source articles: 'NASA’s SpaceX Crew-13 Launches' (image/article) and 'NASA’s SpaceX Crew-13 Launches to International Space Station' (NASA news release).

## Risks

- Crewed launches carry inherent safety and operational risks that could affect reputation if anomalies occur on future missions.
- Ongoing reliance on commercial providers for crew rotation ties NASA ISS operations to the performance and schedule of private launch providers.
- Geopolitical or international partner issues (e.g., involving Roscosmos) could influence mission operations or public perception (not stated as an issue in the sources).
