# NASA’s SpaceX Crew‑12 Splashes Down, Concluding Station Science Mission

The four-person SpaceX Crew‑12 increment (NASA astronauts Jessica Meir and Jack Hathaway, ESA astronaut Sophie Adenot, and Roscosmos cosmonaut Andrey Fedyaev) concluded its mission to the International Space Station with a crewed splashdown in the Pacific Ocean off the coast of California on Oct. 8, 2026. NASA and SpaceX had targeted undocking no earlier than 8:05 a.m. EDT on Wednesday, Oct. 7, 2026, and NASA planned to stream the return and splashdown live. NASA scheduled a crew news conference at Johnson Space Center for Oct. 15 to discuss the mission. Earlier reporting described the return as occurring in "early October 2026," creating variability in reported dates prior to the confirmed splashdown.

## Industry implication

A confirmed, routine splashdown for Crew‑12 reinforces operational confidence in SpaceX’s Crew Dragon crew transport and recovery processes and supports near‑term public and programmatic confidence in commercial crew services and ISS utilization. Immediate commercial or financial impacts are limited; reputational and operational continuity effects are primary.

## Key facts

- SpaceX Crew‑12 crew members were NASA astronauts Jessica Meir and Jack Hathaway, ESA astronaut Sophie Adenot, and Roscosmos cosmonaut Andrey Fedyaev.
- NASA and SpaceX targeted undocking no earlier than 8:05 a.m. EDT on Wednesday, Oct. 7, 2026, for the Crew‑12 spacecraft.
- The Crew‑12 spacecraft (Crew Dragon Freedom) splashed down in the Pacific Ocean off the coast of California on Oct. 8, 2026.
- NASA intended to stream the Crew‑12 return and splashdown live.
- NASA scheduled a news conference with the Crew‑12 crew at 3:30 p.m. EDT on Thursday, Oct. 15, at Johnson Space Center to discuss the mission.
- The crew spent more than seven months aboard the International Space Station prior to return.
