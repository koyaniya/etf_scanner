# NASA’s SpaceX 35th Commercial Resupply Mission Overview

NASA and SpaceX are targeting the launch of SpaceX’s 35th commercial resupply services mission to the International Space Station on Oct. 13, 2026 (target time reported as 6:33 a.m. EDT). The mission will deliver science investigations, supplies, equipment and—according to one linked release—the final set of International Space Station Roll-Out Solar Arrays; NASA set media coverage for the launch and arrival.

## Industry implication

A scheduled CRS cargo launch maintains the ISS resupply cadence and supports NASA science and station operations in the near term. Delivery of the final set of Roll-Out Solar Arrays (per one report) would support an ISS infrastructure upgrade; the announcement and scheduled coverage reinforce SpaceX’s operational role under NASA’s CRS program.

## Key facts

- Article title: 'NASA’s SpaceX 35th Commercial Resupply Mission Overview.'
- The flight is identified as SpaceX's 35th commercial resupply services mission to the International Space Station for NASA.
- Sources report the planned launch date as Oct. 13, 2026.
- One analysis reports a target launch time of 6:33 a.m. EDT on Tuesday, Oct. 13, 2026.
- The mission will deliver science investigations, supplies, and equipment to the ISS.
- One source states the mission will deliver the final set of International Space Station Roll-Out Solar Arrays.
- NASA set media coverage for the launch and arrival (per one report).

## Risks

- The launch date and time are targets (phrasing includes 'no earlier than' and a specific target time) and could change due to weather, technical issues, or range/operational holds.
- Delays or a failed launch would postpone delivery of listed cargo, including the Roll-Out Solar Arrays if present.
