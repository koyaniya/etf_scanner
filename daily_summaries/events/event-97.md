# NASA’s SSPICY Mission to Demonstrate In-Space Inspection Technologies

NASA-supported Small Spacecraft Propulsion and Inspection Capability (SSPICY) mission launched Oct. 1 from Vandenberg Space Force Base to demonstrate in-space inspection technologies for approaching defunct satellites to support future repairs and reduce orbital debris.

## Industry implication

If successful, SSPICY could advance technologies needed for orbital servicing and debris mitigation, lowering technical risk for future in‑space repair and inspection services and supporting growth of orbital servicing capabilities for government and commercial actors.

## Key facts

- The mission is named the Small Spacecraft Propulsion and Inspection Capability (SSPICY).
- The SSPICY mission is NASA-supported.
- The mission launched Oct. 1 from Vandenberg Space Force Base in California.
- The mission is a technology demonstration to get up close to satellites that are no longer in service to demonstrate in-space inspection technologies intended to support future in-space repairs and reduce orbital debris.

## Risks

- Technology-demonstration risk: the mission may fail to demonstrate the intended inspection capabilities.
- Operational and safety risk for proximity operations around defunct satellites could limit applicability or require additional safeguards.
- Even if technically successful, scaling demonstration results into routine commercial or government orbital servicing operations may face regulatory, operational, or cost barriers.
